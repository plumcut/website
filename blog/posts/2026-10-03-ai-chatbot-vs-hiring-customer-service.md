An AI chatbot is cheaper than hiring customer service staff in Lebanon and GCC markets such as Saudi Arabia and the UAE when questions repeat, since one hire covers 48 of 168 weekly hours.

Messages are piling up, replies are going out late, and the obvious fix is to hire someone. Before you post the job ad, run the numbers properly, because the cost of a customer service hire is not the salary on the offer letter, and one hire does not cover the hours your customers are actually messaging.

> For repetitive customer questions, an AI chatbot is almost always cheaper than hiring. A week has 168 hours, and the labour laws of Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Lebanon cap a standard working week at 48 hours, so keeping one seat filled around the clock takes about three and a half people before anyone takes leave. Each hire then adds employer costs: social insurance, an end of service payment, paid leave and recruitment. An AI like plum answers at every hour for one fixed monthly fee. Hiring still wins for judgment work, refunds, exceptions and complaints, which is why the best answer is usually AI for the volume and people for the decisions.

## The number most people get wrong: coverage

Your customers do not message in office hours. They message after dinner, on Friday, during Ramadan nights. A hire covers their shift and nothing else.

| Country | Standard maximum working week | Hires needed to cover 168 hours | Paid annual leave on top |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Saudi Arabia | [48 hours](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/en/knowledge-centre/articles/312), 36 in Ramadan for Muslim employees | 3.5, or 4.7 in Ramadan | [At least 21 days](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/en/knowledge-centre/articles/321), 30 after five years |
| UAE | [8 hours a day or 48 a week](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/Sector-of-employment/employment-in-the-private-sector/working-hours) | 3.5 | 30 days after one year of service |
| Lebanon | [48 hours](https://www.nrc.no/globalassets/pdf/guidelines/guide-to-employment-rights/lebanon-employment-rights-guide---english.pdf) under the Labour Code | 3.5 | 15 days after one year |

So the honest comparison is never "one salary against one subscription". It is either three and a half salaries against one subscription, or one salary plus a promise to customers that nobody answers after six. Most brands quietly choose the second, and pay for it in carts that go cold overnight. We looked at what that silence costs in [what happens when your store gets more messages than your team can read](/blog/too-many-customer-messages-retail).

## What a hire costs beyond the salary

Every country in the region adds employer costs that never appear on the offer letter. Checked on official or published legal sources on 3 October 2026; this is a guide, not payroll advice.

**Saudi Arabia.** For a Saudi employee, the employer pays GOSI [annuities at 9 percent](https://www.gosi.gov.sa/GOSIOnline/FAQ_Employer?locale=en_US) for existing contributors, and GOSI's own guidance says the rate for new entrants under the new Social Insurance Law rises in half point steps to 11 percent. On top of that, [2 percent for occupational hazards](https://www.gosi.gov.sa/GOSIOnline/FAQ_Employer?locale=en_US), which also applies to non-Saudi employees, and [0.75 percent for SANED](https://www.gosi.gov.sa/GOSIOnline/FAQ_Unemployment_Insurance_(SANED)?locale=en_US) unemployment insurance. The [Labor Law](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2023-02/Labor.pdf) adds an end of service award of half a month's wage for each of the first five years and a full month for each year after.

**United Arab Emirates.** The [end of service gratuity](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/Sector-of-employment/employment-in-the-private-sector/end-of-service-benefits-for-employees-in-the-private-sector) is 21 days of salary for each of the first five years and 30 days for each year after, capped at two years of wages. If you hire an Emirati, the private sector [minimum wage is AED 6,000 a month](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/Sector-of-employment/employment-in-the-private-sector/emiratis-employment-in-private-sector) from 1 January 2026.

**Lebanon.** A published legal guide to Lebanese employment rights lists employer NSSF contributions of 8.5 percent for end of service indemnity, 6 percent for family allowances and 8 percent for sickness and maternity, about 22.5 percent in total before wage ceilings. The private sector minimum wage was [raised to LBP 28 million a month](https://wageindicator.org/work/minimum-wage/updates/2025/minimum-wage-updated-in-lebanon-from-01-august-2025-august-08-2025/) in mid 2025.

Then add what no law lists: recruiting, the weeks of training before a new hire answers confidently, a manager's time, a laptop and a phone line, and starting over when they leave.

## Run it for your own store

An illustrative method, not a measured result. Plug in your own salary figure; we are deliberately not quoting market salaries, because they vary too much by city and seniority to be useful.

1. **Monthly salary** of one customer service hire: call it S.
2. **Employer on-costs**: in Lebanon, roughly 22.5 percent NSSF from the guide above. In Saudi Arabia, for a Saudi hire, about 11.75 percent rising toward 13.75 percent with GOSI, plus the end of service accrual. In the UAE, the gratuity alone accrues at 21 days of salary for every year of the first five.
3. **Coverage**: multiply by 3.5 if you want every hour covered, or accept that one hire means one shift.
4. **Hidden overhead**: recruitment, training and management time, honestly estimated.

A single Lebanese hire on salary S therefore costs about 1.225 S a month before overhead, and round the clock cover costs about 4.3 S. Compare that with a fixed monthly AI fee plus Meta's own message charges, and the gap is usually not close. To price the AI side properly, line by line, see [what AI customer service really costs](/blog/ai-customer-service-hidden-costs).

## What an AI does better, and what a person does better

| Job | AI on WhatsApp | A trained person |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Is this in stock, how much, do you deliver to my area" | Instant, at any hour, from live data | Accurate, but only in their shift |
| The same question for the hundredth time today | Same quality the hundredth time | Quality drops by evening |
| Arabic, English and Arabizi in one chat | Handles all three when built for it | Depends on the hire |
| A sudden flood of messages after a campaign | Answers them all at once | Queue forms |
| Refunds, exceptions, goodwill | Should hand over | Should decide |
| An angry customer you let down | Should hand over, fast | The right person for it |

Meta's [WhatsApp Business messaging policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/) allows automated replies in the 24 hour customer service window but requires prompt, clear and direct escalation to a person. So the realistic design is not AI instead of people. It is AI for the volume and your existing team for the moments that need judgment, which usually means you do not need the extra hire at all.
