Yes, you can send abandoned cart messages on WhatsApp, as long as the customer opted in and the message uses a template approved in Meta's marketing category.

Someone filled a cart, got as far as the delivery step, and vanished. You have their number from a previous order. Sending them a nudge on WhatsApp feels obvious, and it is one of the fastest ways to get your number blocked if you do it wrong.

> You can send abandoned cart messages on WhatsApp, but only to people who opted in, and only through a template Meta has approved as **marketing**. Meta lists cart abandonment reminders under Marketing in its own template categorization docs, alongside promotions and retargeting, so any advice to file them as Utility to get a cheaper rate is simply wrong. Utility means following up on a user action, like an order or delivery update. Opt in is required before you message anyone at all, it does not have to be WhatsApp specific, but it must name your business. Having someone's number from a past purchase is not opt in, and neither is a pre-ticked box. Get the consent, submit the template as marketing, and pay the marketing rate.

## The two rules that actually matter

Everything else about cart recovery on WhatsApp is tactics. These two are the ones that get accounts restricted.

### 1. Opt in comes first, always

Meta requires opt in before you message anyone on WhatsApp. Two details people get wrong:

- **It does not have to be WhatsApp specific.** A general marketing consent works, as long as you comply with local law.
- **It must name your business.** The person has to know who they are agreeing to hear from. "Receive updates" on an unbranded checkbox does not clear this.

What is not opt in: having the number from a past order, a pre-ticked box, scraping, or a list someone sold you. Every one of those is how numbers get reported and blocked.

### 2. A cart reminder is a marketing template

This is where most of the internet gets it wrong, including several guides published this year.

Meta's own [template categorization documentation](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/templates/template-categorization) lists the categories like this:

| Category | What it covers | Meta's own examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Marketing** | Awareness, sales, retargeting | New product announcements, targeted promotions and offers, **cart abandonment reminders** |
| **Utility** | Following up on a user action or request | Opt in confirmation, order and delivery updates, payment reminders, feedback surveys |
| **Authentication** | Verifying identity | One time passcodes, login verification |

Cart abandonment is named explicitly under Marketing. Not implied, listed.

So when a guide tells you to send the first reminder without a discount so it stays Utility and costs less, it is advising you to misclassify a template. That is not a clever optimisation. It is a category dispute waiting to happen, and template categorisation is reviewed rather than taken on trust.

The honest version is simpler: **a cart reminder is marketing, budget for the marketing rate.**

## Why the distinction is worth real money

Marketing is the most expensive category, and Meta bills per message delivered rather than per conversation since July 2025. If cart recovery is a meaningful part of your volume, that line grows quickly, which is exactly why people go looking for a way to file it as something cheaper.

There is a legitimate way to spend less, and it is not misclassification.

**The 24 hour customer service window.** When a customer messages you, a window opens for 24 hours during which you can reply without a template. Since 1 October 2026 those service replies are free for the first 1,000 per business phone number each month and then charged at the market's service rate, per [Meta's pricing page](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing). A customer who abandons a cart and then messages you a question is already inside that window. Answering them well, immediately, costs at most the service rate and converts better than any template you could have sent.

**Free entry points.** When someone messages you from a click to WhatsApp ad on the WhatsApp Android or iOS app, your first reply inside the customer service window opens a free entry point window that, per [Meta's pricing page](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing), can stay open for up to 7 days. Inside it, marketing, utility, authentication and service messages are not charged, though Meta Business Agent messages still are. If you run paid social, routing that traffic into WhatsApp changes the arithmetic of cart recovery entirely, because the recovery conversation happens inside the free window rather than as a paid template.

The pattern is the same in both cases. **Conversations you are invited into are cheap. Conversations you start are expensive.** Build for the first.

## What a defensible setup looks like

1. **A real opt in at checkout**, separate from your order updates consent, naming your business. Two checkboxes, not one. Order updates and marketing are different permissions and conflating them is the most common mistake.
2. **A marketing template**, submitted as marketing, approved before you need it. Approval takes time and rejections cost days, so do not build the campaign first.
3. **One reminder, not a sequence.** Repeatedly messaging someone who did not reply is what generates blocks, and blocks damage your quality rating, which limits how much you can send at all.
4. **A reply path that goes to a person or something that behaves like one.** Most abandoned carts are a question the customer never asked out loud: does this fit, when does it arrive, can I return it. A reminder that just says "you left something" answers none of them.
5. **Somewhere to read what came back.** The replies to cart reminders are the cheapest customer research you will ever get, and almost everyone deletes them.

## The part most brands skip

Cart recovery is usually treated as a sending problem. It is a conversation problem.

The reminder is easy. What decides whether the sale closes is what happens in the ninety seconds after the customer replies, which is often at eleven at night when nobody is watching the inbox. A recovered cart is not a delivered template, it is an answered question.

That is the same reason [answering fast matters more than messaging more](/blog/how-to-automate-whatsapp-for-your-business), and it is why the cost of the template is rarely the number that decides whether this is worth doing. ## What to do this week

Check your checkout for a marketing opt in that names your business and is separate from order updates. If it is not there, nothing else on this page is available to you legally, and that is the whole project this week.

Then count how many carts you abandon in a month and multiply by your average basket. That number tells you whether to build this at all, and most brands have never calculated it.
