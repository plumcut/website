To upsell inside a customer conversation, make one offer at one of four moments: after a stated need, after the order is confirmed, when an objection appears, or after delivery.

Most retail upselling happens on a page where nobody is talking. A widget under the product image, a cart drawer, an offer wedged between order confirmation and the thank you page. It works well enough. But if a real share of your orders starts with someone messaging you to ask a question, then the place your customer actually said what they want is a conversation, and almost nothing on the market sells there.

> There are four moments in a conversation worth an offer, and they are not equally good. Best is after the customer states a need but before they pick a product, because a recommendation is literally what they asked for. Second is right after they confirm, where a genuinely complementary item adds to a decision instead of questioning it. Third is when an objection appears that a different product actually answers. Fourth is after delivery, once the thing arrived and they are happy. One offer per moment, and a no ends it. The moment that always backfires is offering anything while a question of theirs is still unanswered.

## The four moments, ranked

| Moment | Offer type | Why it works | What kills it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Need stated, product not yet chosen | A recommendation, including a better option | You were asked for advice, so advice is not an interruption | Recommending something that ignores the stated need or budget |
| Just confirmed the purchase | A complementary item | Adds to the decision rather than reopening it | Offering something more expensive, which reads as second guessing |
| An objection appears | A different product that answers the objection | Solves the actual blocker instead of arguing with it | Pushing the original item harder |
| After delivery, order went well | A refill, a matching piece, a reorder | Goodwill is at its highest point and the product proved itself | Sending it before you know the delivery went well |

The ranking is the useful part. Most stores put all their effort into moment two, the classic add on at checkout, because that is where the tooling lives. Moment one converts better and needs no tooling at all, only someone who reads what the customer wrote.

## What to say, concretely

Vague advice about being helpful is useless, so here are the shapes.

**Moment one, a need stated.** The customer says what they are looking for and roughly what they want to spend. The reply names two or three real options within that range, says plainly what separates them, and includes one slightly better option with the reason attached. Not "we also have a premium version" but "the larger one is 20 percent more and lasts about twice as long, which people buying it for daily use usually prefer". The offer is the reason, not the product. In grocery the stated need is often a whole meal, which is why this moment does so much work there; we walked through it in [how to upsell groceries on WhatsApp](/blog/upsell-groceries-whatsapp).

**Moment two, just confirmed.** One item, obviously related, mentioned once. "Most people order the refill with this so they do not run out in a month, want me to add it?" If the answer is no, it is closed. Do not restate it in the confirmation message.

**Moment three, an objection.** The customer says it is too expensive, or the size is wrong, or delivery is too slow to their area. Each of those is answerable with a different product rather than with persuasion. A cheaper model, a different size, an item that ships from closer. Treating an objection as a product question rather than a sales obstacle is the whole technique.

**Moment four, after delivery.** This one has a rule attached. Only send it once you know the order actually arrived and nothing went wrong, which means it has to read your order and courier data. Sending a reorder prompt to someone whose parcel was lost is worse than sending nothing at all.

## Where helpful turns into pushy

Three limits, and two of them are other people's rules rather than our opinion.

**A declined offer ends the sequence.** Not later in the same conversation, not in the confirmation message. Once.

**Three consecutive offers is the documented ceiling, and a conversation should stay well under it.** Shopify's own developer guidance for [post-purchase product offers](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/checkout/product-offers/ux-for-post-purchase-product-offers) is to display a maximum of three consecutive upsell offers and to tailor them to the customer's actual behaviour. That is guidance for a page, where the customer is clicking rather than talking. In a live conversation there is a person on the other side and the practical limit is one.

**Reaching back out later is a different thing with different rules.** An offer inside a live conversation the customer started sits in the 24 hour customer service window, and non-template service messages there are free for the first 1,000 per business phone number each month and then charged at the market's service rate, under [Meta's pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing) since 1 October 2026. Messaging someone after that window closes, a cart reminder or a reorder prompt, is a marketing message on the WhatsApp Business Platform. It needs opt-in and an approved marketing template, and it is charged per delivered message. We went through exactly where that line sits in [the rules on WhatsApp cart reminders](/blog/whatsapp-abandoned-cart-rules).

The cost consequence is worth sitting with. The offer made during a live conversation is the cheap one and the one that converts best. The one you pay for is the one you send to someone who already left.

## How to automate it, and what it has to be connected to

An offer is only as good as what the system knows when it makes it. Four connections, in order of how much they matter.

**Live stock.** Offering something out of stock is worse than not offering. This is the one that breaks most often.

**The product catalogue with real attributes.** Size, price, what goes with what. Without relationships between products, a recommendation is a guess dressed up as advice.

**Order history.** It turns moment four from a broadcast into a reorder that makes sense, and it lets you stop offering someone the thing they already bought.

**Courier status.** The gate on moment four. No confirmed delivery, no offer.

On Shopify, WooCommerce and BigCommerce these connections are well trodden. If you run on Salla or Zid, both publish merchant APIs, but neither appeared on the Gorgias, Zendesk, Intercom, Siena or Tidio integration listings when we checked on 13 September 2026, so plan for a custom connection.

## What to do this week

1. Read your last fifty conversations and mark where an offer would have been natural. You will find moment one is the most common and the least used.
2. Write down five product pairs. What genuinely goes with what, in your catalogue, in your customers' words rather than your category names.
3. Check whether your stock data is live. If it is not, fix that before you automate a single offer.
4. Count how many of your orders started with a message rather than a click. That number is how much of your upsell opportunity your current tooling cannot reach.
