Click to WhatsApp Ads: What Happens After the Click

A click to WhatsApp ad does one job: it opens a chat. Everything that decides whether you make money happens after that, inside a conversation most brands never look at. When a business tells us its WhatsApp ads "don't convert", the ad is rarely the problem. The chat is.

This guide covers what happens between the tap and the sale, what Meta changed in its pricing in late 2026, and which parts of the conversation are worth automating.

The click is not the lead

Ads Manager reports "messaging conversations started". That number counts people who tapped and sent a first message, often the prefilled "Hi, I want to know more". It does not count people who got an answer, people who asked a price and left, or people who bought.

Between the click and the sale there are four places a customer drops:

The first reply. The customer writes at 11:40 at night after seeing your ad. If the first answer comes the next morning, the intent is gone. They have already messaged two competitors who were faster.

The first real question. Most ad chats open with a price, a size, a branch or a delivery area. A greeting followed by "how can I help?" adds a turn and loses people. The answer has to be the reply.

The moment of choice. The customer is interested but undecided. This is where a helpful suggestion (the bundle, the larger size, the booking slot tonight) turns interest into an order.

The follow up. Many people say "I'll think about it". Whether anyone comes back to them decides a large share of the sales the ad paid for.

If you only measure cost per conversation, you can't see any of this. A campaign with cheaper chats can sell less than one with expensive chats that get answered properly.

What Meta changed in 2026

Two pricing changes make the post-click conversation more important than it was a year ago.

Service replies now cost money. From 1 October 2026, Meta charges per message for service messages, the normal free-form replies your team or AI sends inside the 24-hour window. Each business number gets 1,000 free service messages a month, and after that every reply is billed. Utility templates sent in reply to a customer are also charged again. In practice, long conversations with many short replies now cost more than short conversations that answer in one complete message.

The free entry point window is the exception. When a customer messages you from a click to WhatsApp ad on the WhatsApp mobile app, and you reply within 24 hours, Meta opens a free entry point window from the moment you reply. Inside it, marketing, utility, authentication and service messages are not charged. Meta's pricing documentation now says this window can stay open for "up to 7 days". Earlier documentation and many providers still describe it as 72 hours. Either way, three conditions decide whether you get it:

  • The chat must start on the WhatsApp Android or iOS app. Desktop and web do not open the window.
  • You must reply inside the first 24 hours. No reply, no free window.
  • Meta Business Agent messages are still charged by token inside the window. Third party AI replies are not charged by Meta inside it, though your provider may charge for them.

So the conversations your ads bring in are now the cheapest conversations you'll ever have with a customer, but only if someone answers quickly.

What a good post-click conversation looks like

Take a supermarket running a Ramadan basket ad in Beirut, or a fashion brand in Dubai promoting a new collection. A strong ad conversation usually has five parts.

An instant first answer that matches the ad. If the ad promoted a basket, the first reply talks about the basket: what is in it, the price, delivery areas. The customer should never have to repeat what they just saw.

One complete answer per question. Price, stock, branch, delivery time and payment options in one message, not spread across four. With service messages now billed, fewer turns mean a lower bill and a faster customer.

A relevant offer at the decision point. Not a catalogue dump. One suggestion that belongs in the basket or the booking, offered once.

A clean handover. When the customer asks for something only a person can do, a staff member takes over with the full context, not a fresh "how can I help?".

A follow up inside the free window. A reminder on day two or three, while it still costs nothing, recovers a share of the "I'll think about it" customers who would otherwise vanish.

What to automate and what to keep human

Automation pays most where volume is high and the answer is known:

  • First replies at any hour, in Arabic, English or the Arabizi mix customers actually write in
  • Prices, stock, sizes, branches, opening hours and delivery areas
  • Building the order or booking and sending it to your system
  • Follow ups and reminders inside the free entry point window

Keep a person on:

  • Complaints and refunds
  • Custom or high-value orders
  • Anything where the customer asks for a human

The aim is not to remove your team from ad conversations. It is to make sure no paid conversation waits overnight, and that your team spends its time on the chats where a human closes the sale.

Measure the conversation, not just the ad

Track these alongside your Ads Manager numbers:

  • Time to first reply for ad conversations, by hour of day
  • Share of ad chats that got a complete answer to their first question
  • Orders or bookings per 100 ad conversations
  • Share of follow ups sent inside the free window
  • What people asked that you couldn't answer (often a product you don't stock or a delivery area you don't cover)

The last one is the most underrated. Ad conversations are customer research you've already paid for. Read across them and they tell you what your market wants, which objections kill the sale, and which promotions bring buyers rather than browsers.

The bottom line

If your click to WhatsApp ads are not converting, look at the chat before you change the creative. Answer instantly, answer completely, make one relevant offer, follow up while it is free, and read what customers are telling you. That is where the budget is won or lost.
